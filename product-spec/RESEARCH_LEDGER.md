# Research ledger: traditions, evidence, and trust

Updated: 6 October 2026. This is a source map, not a claim that 100 books have been read or that numerology has scientific predictive validity.

## Findings that affect product design

1. **Modern systems should not be presented as one unchanged ancient science.** The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes the historical development of Pythagorean doctrines, including later Neopythagorean layers; it is a history of philosophy, not a validation of modern birth-chart predictions. The app should name the exact contemporary calculation convention rather than borrow ancient authority for it.
2. **The modern name-and-birthdate practice should not be backdated to Pythagoras.** Andrew Gregory's scholarly chapter, “The Pythagoreans: Number and Numerology,” explicitly distinguishes modern letter/name-plus-birthday numerology from the historical Pythagoreans and cautions against presenting the modern practice as theirs. The UI calls the options “Pythagorean-style” and “Chaldean-style” name mappings and identifies them as table conventions; it does not endorse an ancient lineage.
   The current audit has not established an ancient provenance for the Chaldean-style letter table either; the product describes the implemented values without repeating an origin story as fact.
3. **A direct test of one birth-number claim found no support in its chosen sample.** Genovese (2017), “A Test of Numerology: Do Birth Numbers Predict Nobel Prize Winners?”, reported that Nobel winners' birth-number distribution did not differ significantly from chance. This tests a narrow population/distribution claim; it does not test every numerology school, and it does not establish that numerology works.
4. **Generic statements can feel personally accurate.** Forer's 1949 personal-validation demonstration is a reason to avoid broad, flattering or frightening copy as proof of individual accuracy.
5. **Forecast sharpness is not the same as forecast reliability.** Gneiting & Raftery (2007) discuss sharpness subject to calibration for probabilistic forecasts. This is a useful evaluation principle, not evidence for numerology. The app has no outcome dataset, so it must not claim a hit rate or probability today.
6. **Source traditions differ.** Pythagorean-style letter mapping, Chaldean-style mapping, Lo Shu, and Vedic/planetary associations must be separately named and versioned. A UI that blends them needs to show each contribution and disagreement, not call the blend “ancient verified truth.”
7. **Competitor positioning check (observed 6 Oct 2026):** Clickastro's official numerology product page offers a sample report, lists more than six pages, supports seven languages, advertises PDF delivery by email/WhatsApp, and showed a ₹499 premium price at time of review. Its official 2027 LifePath page lists more than 28 pages, 12 selected questions, nine languages, and ₹999. These are self-described product features/prices, not audited quality, prediction accuracy, or directly comparable services. They do show that page count, generic life-area coverage, multilingual output, and remedies are already mass-market features—not a defensible ₹1 lakh differentiator by themselves.
8. **Premium tier implication:** A ₹1 lakh report target needs a specified service promise and real operating capability beyond automated text: clearly named calculation methods, personalized evidence intake, transparent uncertainty, the now-confirmed human editorial review, a delivery/revision/support policy, and privacy controls. This is product-design reasoning, not proof customers will pay that price. Human review is confirmed as a product requirement, but reviewer credentials, workflow, and fulfillment are not yet implemented, so the local app correctly labels its output as an unreviewed draft.
9. **Current competitor feature scan (6 Oct 2026):** Numen advertises a complete chart, Pinnacles/cycles, current Personal Year/Month, strengths and shadow, long-form copy, and inspectable calculations. NumeroVedic advertises a 30+ page PDF, a 12-month forecast, Life Path/Expression/Soul Urge, Pinnacles/Challenges, Lo Shu and remedies at a listed ₹499. These are competitors' own product claims, not evidence of depth or predictive accuracy. The useful product lesson is that report length, many number labels, monthly cycles, remedies, and bold predictions are not unique differentiation; Anko Ki Maya must add transparent methods, user-authored context, review, and a useful action/revision loop. We do not copy their wording or unsupported event claims.
10. **Book/source review boundary:** Gregory's open OUP chapter was read for historical context; it discusses how little can be stated with certainty about Pythagoras and warns that later sources are often unreliable. The 2026 Macmillan publisher page lists an anthology of six older numerology works, but a publisher description/table of contents is not a substitute for reading every included work. We will not claim 100 books have been read. Traditional manuals can document what a school teaches; that alone does not establish historical origin or predictive validity. Cheiro's *Book of Numbers* should be treated as a primary source for its own modern Chaldean-style claims, not as proof of ancient Babylonian continuity.
11. **Lo Shu boundary:** The open SAGE article by So et al. (2015) treats Luo Shu as a 3×3 magic-square/matrix tradition and describes its context in Chinese compass-school Feng Shui. It does not validate contemporary birth-date personality verdicts. Therefore, if the grid is added to the paid report, its arithmetic can be checked and its interpretive layer must stay separately labelled as a tradition/reflection.
12. **New monthly-map design:** Competitors list Personal Month/year-ahead modules; the flagship report now computes a current-month-through-next-11-months map using the app's existing `personalMonth` formula. Each card pairs a named symbolic lens with an app-authored question, reversible action, and watch-out. The app explicitly says those planning prompts are not canonical source text or event predictions. This adapts the product format, not competitor copy.

## Use and rights

Read sources for method comparison and historical/evidence context. Maintain our own wording, attribute claims, and do not copy protected interpretive passages. Before incorporating a book's proprietary table or a teacher's method, record the edition, source, permitted-use status, and exact rule; otherwise label it as an unverified school convention or omit it.

## Evaluation protocol to build toward

- Freeze each future planning card with creation time, horizon, observable criterion, and the inputs/method version used; never rewrite it after the outcome.
- Separate user-rated resonance from an observable event outcome. Neither is proof of causation.
- For any later probability forecasts, require an unambiguous event definition and follow-up, show sample size and a naive/base-rate comparator, and report calibration/score only after adequate data. Do not infer cohort accuracy from an individual's “fits” response.
- Keep data local unless the owner approves a privacy/security architecture and customers explicitly consent to any upload or research use.
